If you've never crossed the King Fahd Causeway before, the idea of driving between two countries can sound more complicated than it actually is. In practice, it's a well-marked, well-trodden route that thousands of vehicles use every day. This guide walks through exactly what happens at each stage, so there are no surprises the first time you do it.

## Stage One: Leaving Bahrain

The causeway begins at Al Jasra on Bahrain's western coast. Before you reach the bridge itself, you'll pass through Bahrain's exit immigration and customs post, where each passenger presents their own passport (or GCC national ID, where accepted). This is a standard passport check, not usually a lengthy process outside of peak periods — though Thursday evenings, Friday mornings, and public holidays can see longer queues as GCC weekend travel peaks.

If you're taking a [licensed taxi from Bahrain to Dammam](/taxi-bahrain-to-dammam/), your driver handles the vehicle's own paperwork at this stage while you handle your passport — the two processes run alongside each other rather than one holding up the other.

## Stage Two: The Bridge Itself

Once you clear the Bahrain post, you're on the causeway proper — a 25 km span of bridges and reclaimed islands that opened in 1986 as the first fixed link between Bahrain and Saudi Arabia. The drive across takes roughly 20 to 30 minutes at the posted speed limits, depending on traffic.

Midway across sits a man-made island that marks the midpoint between the two countries. It's also where you'll find the joint facilities used by both nations' border authorities, along with the general amenities available to travellers on a long drive.

## Stage Three: Entering Saudi Arabia

At the far end of the bridge, you'll clear Saudi Arabia's entry immigration and customs post. As with the Bahrain side, this involves presenting your own passport and any visa or entry permit your nationality requires — see our [guide to the documents required for this crossing](/blog/documents-required-bahrain-to-saudi-by-road/) if you're not sure what applies to you.

From here, it's a relatively short drive to Khobar, the nearest Saudi city to the crossing, or continue on to Dammam, Jubail, and the rest of the Eastern Province.

## How Long Does the Whole Crossing Take?

Door-to-door, most passengers experience a combined 30 to 40 minutes across both immigration posts on a normal day, plus the 20 to 30 minute bridge drive itself — so budget around an hour just for the crossing, before adding whatever driving time you need on either side to reach your actual destination.

## A Few Practical Notes

The causeway is a vehicle crossing only — there's no pedestrian lane for general passenger traffic, so you'll need to be in a car, taxi, or bus the whole way across. A toll applies to private vehicles, though if you're booking a taxi this is typically built into the fare rather than charged separately at the barrier.

If your trip is specifically an exit-and-return rather than a one-way journey — commonly known as a [visa U-turn or exit-re-entry run](/visa-u-turn-service/) — the same crossing process applies in both directions, just doubled.

Whichever direction you're travelling, the fundamentals don't change: one passport check per country, one bridge in between, and a straightforward process once you know what to expect at each stage.
