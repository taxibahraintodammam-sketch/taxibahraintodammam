Bahrain is a genuinely popular weekend destination for residents of Dammam and Khobar, and the causeway crossing means a short 70 to 90 minute drive puts you in an entirely different country. Here's a rough guide to filling a weekend once you arrive.

## Manama's Old Town and Bab Al Bahrain

Start in central Manama around Bab Al Bahrain, the historic gateway building that marks the entrance to the traditional souq. The surrounding streets are worth wandering for textiles, gold, spices, and the general atmosphere of one of the Gulf's older trading districts.

## Bahrain National Museum

For a sense of the island's history — from ancient Dilmun civilisation through to the pearling era — the Bahrain National Museum on the Manama waterfront is one of the region's better-regarded cultural institutions, with well-curated exhibits that don't require a full day to see properly.

## Adliya's Restaurant Scene

Bahrain's dining reputation centres heavily on Adliya, particularly the streets around Block 338, which has one of the highest concentrations of restaurants and cafes on the island. It's a good choice for a weekend evening, especially if you're staying nearby or don't mind a short taxi ride from your hotel.

## Seef for Shopping

If shopping is part of your weekend plan, Seef is Bahrain's main retail district, home to two of the island's largest malls. It's also one of the more convenient areas for a return trip toward the causeway afterward — see our [pickup guide for Seef](/pickup/seef/) if you're planning your return journey from this area specifically.

## Amwaj Islands for a Change of Pace

For something quieter than central Manama, Amwaj Islands offers a marina, waterfront dining, and a noticeably different atmosphere from the city centre — a reasonable half-day addition if your weekend has room for it.

## Planning the Logistics

A weekend trip generally works best as a [round trip taxi booking](/taxi-dammam-to-bahrain/) — arriving on Thursday evening or Friday morning and returning Saturday or Sunday. Keep in mind that Thursday evening and Friday morning are also the busiest border crossing times, so build in extra time on the way there, and expect the return leg on Saturday or Sunday evening to be busier too.

If you're travelling as a family or larger group, a [van](/family-van-transfer/) keeps everyone together for the crossing and the weekend rather than splitting across vehicles, which tends to make coordinating a group itinerary considerably easier.

## A Practical Note

Whatever your itinerary, remember that entry requirements into Bahrain depend on your own nationality and residency status — confirm what applies to you before travelling, since this varies and changes over time, and isn't something a transport booking resolves on its own.
